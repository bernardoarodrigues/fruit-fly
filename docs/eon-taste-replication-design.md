# Eon-inspired taste and locomotor-context assay

We can test the published taste/locomotor interface with the **existing full male graph and unchanged neural weights, dynamics, body and readout gains**. The most specific next experiment is a small P9-stimulation × leg-taste panel based on Eon's public neural notebook. It should retain sensory-only controls and explicitly distinguish imposed P9 activity from spontaneous sensory-driven walking. No experiment or runtime change was made for this design review.

Eon's March 10 account describes taste-guided movement, a Shiu-derived brain, NeuroMechFly/MuJoCo, 15 ms exchanges, and a small motor interface using oDN1, DNa01/02 and MN9. It states that sensory and motor mappings were chosen by hand. Its description does not give the demo's complete sensory IDs, spatial taste-field/contact law, stimulus amplitudes, motor filters/gains, baseline drive, seed or initialization. Those omissions prevent literal reconstruction of the embodied trajectory; they do not prevent a bounded comparison of a published neural protocol. [Eon article](https://eon.systems/updates/embodied-brain-emulation).

**A concrete source protocol exists.** The separately acquired [public notebook at `c976c7a9`](https://github.com/eonsystemspbc/drosophila_brain_model_lif/blob/c976c7a9/results/eon_1/demo_notebook.ipynb) identifies separate P9 and oDN1 pairs. Cell 9 stimulates both P9s at 100 Hz. Cells 14–16 add twelve `lgAG2` neurons and two ascending-leg neurons at 200 Hz. Cells 35–36 stimulate either ascending-leg neuron at 200 Hz **while retaining bilateral P9 stimulation at 100 Hz**. These unilateral calls are not sensory-only. Its notebook labels and protocol are neural assays, not a released specification of the physical demonstration.

The current [benchmark at `a3db62f`](https://github.com/eonsystemspbc/fly-brain/blob/a3db62f/code/benchmark.py) instead lists 21 sugar GRNs at 200 Hz and a separate P9 benchmark at 100 Hz. That sugar list differs from the older notebook's 23-cell sugar list. Neither should silently replace the notebook's fourteen-cell leg cohort. Exact source lists, revisions and differences belong to the parallel [repository extraction receipt](../validation/eon-public-code-extracted.json). This review independently read the relevant downloaded notebook cells and benchmark definitions; it downloaded or executed neither repository.

Our retained graph is MaleCNS v1.0: 166,700 neurons, 25,582,938 directed edges and 124,177,617 retained synaptic contacts. It includes the VNC and is not the source FlyWire graph. A male transfer therefore remains an **Eon-inspired assay**, even if source stimulus frequencies are copied exactly. Keep the graph, sign assignment, 0.275 mV/contact scale and Shiu parameters unchanged. A graph edge count must not be compared directly with the source's synaptic-contact count.

The male graph contains eleven `LgAG2` cells; their exact IDs and leg assignments are already retained in the [sensory mapping audit](sensory-mapping-evidence.md). The completed [ascending-leg crosswalk](eon-leg-afferent-crosswalk.md) assigns the two source cells to AN_GNG_162, with eight MaleCNS type-level candidates and no verified one-to-one pair. Notebook leg-side labels differ from released nerve-entry sides; the represented receptive field remains unresolved. Do not assume those two are `LgLG4`, use all leg cells as their substitutes, or claim a complete fourteen-to-male correspondence while that check is pending. A known-`LgAG2` subset assay is executable now if labeled as such; an expanded source-cohort transfer should freeze the verified additional identities before running.

The existing leg mapping follows the retained audit of [Tastekin et al., Cell 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.016). It distinguishes leg inputs from labellar LB3b/c and pharyngeal cells. A fresh Cell fetch in this review returned 403, so the exact published assignments rely on that earlier saved source audit and current annotation checks. No evidence was found here for treating all `LB3*` cells as sweet or stimulating pharyngeal cells on foot contact. Shiu's paper tests GRN activation, feeding-circuit recruitment and MN9/proboscis-extension responses; it does not turn a fixed input event rate into a measured concentration-response law. [Shiu et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07763-9).

**Existing taste evidence supports doing this before altering general inhibition.** In the retained [NMF taste-contact result](../validation/taste-contact/results.json), the 0.5 s taste condition ends with smoothed right/left steering rates of **3.41366 / 0.07545 Hz**, forward rate **0.003504 Hz**, commands **+0.06685 / −0.06668**, **0.58596 mm net planar displacement**, and **0.009432 normalized intake**. No-taste and sweet-output-blocked conditions have zero decoder output at the endpoint and zero intake. These are specific saved model outcomes, not a demonstrated heading toward food. Motor mute changes subsequent contacts and input histories; its divergent neural activity is not evidence that muting directly changes the neural equations. The [matched-afferent follow-up](feeding-motor-expansion.md) addresses that confound.

**Immediate proposed panel.** Use seeds 11 and 12, 0.5 s per condition, the same full graph and existing Shiu model, initially without a new body run:

| Condition | Bilateral male P9 input | Verified male leg-input cohort |
|---|---:|---:|
| No imposed activity | 0 | 0 |
| Taste only | 0 | 200 Hz/cell |
| P9 context only | 100 Hz/cell | 0 |
| P9 plus taste | 100 Hz/cell | 200 Hz/cell |

These frequencies come from the notebook protocol, not a fit to male movement. Start with the known `LgAG2` mapping unless the ascending-pair audit closes before freezing the plan. Record the cohort label and IDs explicitly. Compare oDN1, individual DNa01/02, MN9 and relevant source-cell spike counts, event timing and voltage/synaptic traces in every condition; an absent or opposite response is a valid result. A following left-versus-right ascending-input comparison is especially useful once those identities are established. Do not identify steering direction from a neuron name alone.

The notebook's **stored output tables**, inspected in cells 11, 18 and 38, already have an asymmetric P9-only baseline: DNa02 left/right **17.033333 / 6.433333 Hz**, oDN1 **14.833333 / 10.733333 Hz**. Left-leg coactivation gives DNa02 **28.966667 / 6.333333**, right-leg coactivation **11.166667 / 30.033333**; bilateral leg input gives oDN1 **8.800000 / 8.633333**. These are displayed source neural results, not independently rerun spike statistics or measured body speeds. Compare each male stimulus condition to its own matched P9-only baseline, as well as reporting raw rates. Subtracting a baseline for analysis does not authorize installing a hidden steering offset in the motor decoder. Test mirrored inputs and separate seeds before considering any fitted offset; none is identified by the public embodied account.

Use a fixed ordered afferent union across the four numerical conditions, with zero event rates in the inactive channels and the same RNG assignment per source. **Listed zero-rate cells still receive the source API's zero-refractory declaration.** Therefore the no-event control means no imposed events under that common stimulation-eligibility convention; it is not an untouched-resting-physiology claim. Save this declaration and each cell's refractory setting. A separately labeled source-style variable-cohort or truly empty-input control can test that convention without rewriting the primary panel. All event rates must be reported separately from actual generated GRN/P9 spikes. The existing `motor_probe` targets oDN1/DNg97 at 40 Hz and is not this P9 protocol; do not reuse its assay label unmodified or add a direct oDN1 stimulus.

After that neural check, route the same declared stimulation through the existing NMF body and the existing decoder for a short embodied context assay. Movement must still pass **P9 input → full graph → recorded oDN1/steering activity → unchanged decoder → body**. No command override, target-bearing steering or non-neural forward offset is needed. Keep the original 5 ms NMF taste coupling first to avoid adding a simultaneous timing factor; test the source's 15 ms exchange separately if useful. FlyBody currently requires 2 ms exchange and remains another explicitly different body condition.

The decoder currently smooths rates with 50 ms time constant, divides forward activity by 40 Hz, divides the bilateral steering difference by 50 Hz with clipping, and gives feed mode priority when smoothed MN9 exceeds 5 Hz and resource contact exists. These are our declared engineering mappings. In the embodied comparison, record requested modes and pre-priority forward/turn signals: a feeding decision can suppress movement even when steering neurons fire. Preserve the existing gains and feed priority rather than adjusting them until a desired path appears. [Current decoder](../fruitfly/sensors.py).

**Secondary interaction test.** The saved NMF [afferent histories](../validation/feeding-afferent-replay.json) already contain every ordered 5 ms input array for two 0.5 s taste trials. They support a small taste-event on/off × ORN-background 0/5 Hz replay, with P9 unstimulated. Preserve each interval's input list/order, zero the existing taste rates for the off arm, and change only the existing ORN rates for the background arm. The original taste-on/odor-zero neural replay supplies an exact-reference condition. This is a baseline-odor interaction test, not a recreation of the full plume/adaptation history. It should follow the more specific public P9/leg protocol rather than substitute for it.

Our runner always lists **105** DM1/DM4 ORNs, even when both odor gains are zero; inactive leg-taste groups are ordinarily omitted. Simply toggling `enable_taste` changes RNG assignment and refractory declarations. A common fixed replay schedule avoids that hidden contrast. In a subsequent closed loop, real foot contacts will diverge between conditions; save that divergence and use a separate matched-input replay for neural attribution. Sweet-source outgoing blocking and motor-readout mute test different links and should remain distinct controls.

A later genuine navigation assay needs local sensory information before or at its stated detection point. Our current taste is binary actual tarsal resource contact, with no non-contact taste field. A distant isolated patch gives no taste direction before contact. First test contact-triggered stopping, turning and retention near a patch, including mirrored placements, without claiming long-range attraction. If a floor-tastant gradient is later added, sample a separately declared local concentration at actual tarsal contact points and keep it distinct from the consumable resource footprint. That would be an engineering stimulus design, not a recovered Eon field law. No new cue field, oral mechanism or global neural-parameter change is a prerequisite for the immediate bounded panel.
