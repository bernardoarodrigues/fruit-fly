# Recurrent implementation and performance probe

This stage checks whether the four-arm implementation reproduces its original control and measures its execution cost. It precedes the [three-seed stimulus and withdrawal panel](inhibitory-recurrent-design.md). It is not a physiological validation or a model-promotion result.

The [experiment-local kernel](inhibitory-recurrent-kernel.md) retains the two factorial axes: signed current versus inhibitory conductance, and original versus revised synaptic handling during refractory/reset intervals. All cells generate their own recurrent spikes. The fixed graph, direct voltage input, threshold, delay and source refractory convention are preserved. Production neural/body defaults are unchanged; the cancelled Eon embodied integration benchmark remains cancelled.

The [retained-input audit](inhibitory-recurrent-input-audit.md) passes 293 checks over all fifteen earlier Or42a trials. Their first 50 ms contain ordered spikes, stimulus candidates, inferred applied events, RNG boundaries and 5 ms summaries. They do not contain a complete state checkpoint at 50 ms. Therefore this probe adds a fresh original-engine reference, then checks C0's complete voltage/synaptic state, last-spike times, refractory values, source-block mask and valid pending queues at every 5 ms boundary. C0 must also reproduce the archived prefix. The altered arms may run only after these comparisons pass.

The [runner](../scripts/probe_inhibitory_recurrent.py) freezes its plan and source hashes before execution. Five separate 50 ms runs use seed 11 and constant 11 Hz input to the same 36 Or42a sources, in order: original reference, C0, C1, H0, H1. Paired uniform values are generated by independent integer xorshift64* arithmetic outside the experimental kernel. Each arm derives its own applied-input mask and recurrent events. The original reference's applied mask is inferred using the source's zero-refractory, same-tick-spike rejection rule; the experimental kernel records actual writes.

Compilation/warmup, timed kernel calls, checkpoint/telemetry compression and construction are recorded separately. A warmed scalar workload repeats all 125 previously frozen numerical cases 128 times, each with its own timestep and coefficients. This measures a broader range of inhibitory integration costs without inventing a recurrent workload or changing parameters after observing speed.

The execution budget is 600 seconds, 2 GiB of output and 8 GiB peak process RSS. Checks run around each 5 ms chunk; one chunk/archive operation may exceed a limit before the next check. Input hashing precedes the execution timer. Resource exhaustion stops the experiment as an engineering outcome. It does not reject an inhibitory mechanism. Numerical failures, interruptions and any available partial state are retained with their clock/coherence limits; failed networks are not resumed.

Quiet early baseline performance may underestimate later recurrent activity and inhibitory work. This probe does not establish relevant physiological amplitudes/timing, stimulus contrasts, seed robustness or absence of uncontrolled persistence. H1 remains unselected.

## Executed serial probe

The [amended frozen plan](../validation/inhibitory-recurrent-performance-plan-v2.json) has SHA-256 `b8dce6c73dad3f0502ef5c13cc866257a84dc33f9adf5c7b5dd3b62ac17e6360`. Its [results](../validation/inhibitory-recurrent-performance-v2/results.json), SHA-256 `d9a8269b2e24538bb652367c3ac11bcf4c8a5f33b91d4c0f75377fb8b28dac82`, complete all five runs with **280 checks passing**. C0's final full arrays match the fresh original engine byte for byte; every 5 ms full-state digest agrees. Both also reproduce the retained old prefix. The original engine and C0 each produce 113 spikes, with 30 candidate/applied direct arrivals.

All three phases—before threshold, after direct input, and after reset—are included in these extrema. C0's final minimum is −199.601781 mV, so endpoint-only reporting would miss the more extreme excursion. The original reference lacks the experimental per-tick scans and selected event/state logging; its timing is not an equal-instrumentation comparison.

C1's much higher spike count shows that retaining synaptic state changes early recurrent recruitment even when the signed-current equation is unchanged. H0/H1 also generate distinct histories. These are 50 ms startup observations, not a stimulus contrast, evidence of sustained activity after withdrawal, or a preferred arm.

The complete execution took 9.88 s, with about 4.06 MB of retained artifacts and 505 MB peak process RSS at the last budget check. The warmed scalar grid completed 16,000 independent intervals in 0.00868 s, including 13,312 with positive h; its maximum inverse-solver iteration count was three. Kernel work grows within the short H trials: H1's last 5 ms chunk needed 774,298 nonzero-h integrations and 0.738 s, compared with no nonzero-h integrations initially. Extrapolating the entire panel from average startup speed would therefore be misleading.

The reported warmup values include already cached compilation, not a guaranteed cold compiler benchmark. The [two-case preflight record](../validation/inhibitory-recurrent-performance-v2-preflight.json) explicitly records this.

### Preserved compilation failure

The [initial plan](../validation/inhibitory-recurrent-performance-plan.json), [initial runner](../scripts/probe_inhibitory_recurrent.py) and [failed receipt](../validation/inhibitory-recurrent-performance/results.json) are unchanged. Numba could not dynamically index the scalar helper's heterogeneous return tuple. It stopped in warmup, before graph loading or any full-graph arm. The [v2 runner](../scripts/probe_inhibitory_recurrent_v2.py) replaces that operation with seven literal tuple indices and uses separate plan/output paths; the neural kernel, solver, stimulus and numerical protocol are unchanged. The [prospective source review](inhibitory-recurrent-performance-review.md) acknowledges that import/schema checks did not catch this compilation failure.

The subsequent [matched four-thread check](inhibitory-recurrent-parallel-performance.md) passes exact retained-array parity. Next is the separately frozen stimulus/withdrawal panel. No numerical speed approximation or model promotion follows from this probe.

The [independent saved-data review](inhibitory-recurrent-performance-independent-review.md) passes **499 checks**, reconstructing each arm's final spike history/queue, signed edge dispositions, selected event order and synaptic transitions. All 48,000 selected H0/H1 intervals agree with an independent time-domain integral within 1.43e−14 mV. The selected cells happen to remain refractory-available throughout this short probe, all 30 candidates are applied, and no source is blocked. The probe therefore supplies no new observed rejection/blocking case. Full global state arrays exist at endpoints; intervening global checkpoint hashes are producer summaries, not independently recovered arrays.

A later review of the parallel runner identified an additional interruption-path limitation in the frozen serial runner: if the kernel succeeds but allocation of its returned wrapper result fails after the mutable clock advances, that clock may exceed the last confirmed return boundary. The serial probe had no such exception and its successful evidence is unaffected. The new parallel runner records the separately known pre-call boundary for an uncaught exception, while preserving the mutable clock as a distinct, unconfirmed field.
