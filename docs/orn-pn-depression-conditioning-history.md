# What precedes the Fig. 8 conditioning train?

The local Kazama–Wilson paper and supplement establish a disconnected antennal preparation and the 7 Hz conditioning train. They **do not establish that each Fig. 8D–F trial starts from a fully recovered synapse**. The scalar calibration's initial efficacy of one therefore remains a model assumption.

| Evidence | Established fact | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main paper, printed p. 402 / PDF p. 2, preparation description | Antennae were acutely removed and antennal nerve axons electrically stimulated while recording PNs. | This removes the intact peripheral source of ordinary antennal ORN spikes; it does not establish a globally silent circuit. |
| Main paper, printed p. 411 / PDF p. 11, “Stimulation of ORN Axons” | Antennal nerves were severed immediately before recording, near entry into the first antennal segment. | No quantified delay from severing, stabilization or previous stimulation to the first Fig. 8 conditioning pulse is given. |
| Supplement Fig. S3, PDF p. 8 | Acute nerve severing leaves the central ORN projections in place. Miniature EPSCs were studied in this preparation. | Disconnection is not removal of terminals or proof that all spontaneous release is absent. TTX is described for subsequent miniature-current recordings, not as the Fig. 8D–F train condition. |
| Main paper, printed p. 407 / PDF p. 7; Fig. 8D–F caption, printed p. 408 / PDF p. 8 | Each trial begins with stimulation mimicking 7 Hz spontaneous firing; the caption specifies 4 s. The higher-frequency test immediately follows for 500 ms. | “Begins” defines the described trial, not the complete previous stimulation history or a recovery criterion. The precise baseline-to-test pulse phase remains unspecified. |
| Supplement, “Stimulation of ORN axons,” printed pp. 1–2 / PDF pp. 2–3 | Minimal stimulation was selected using recruitment/stability checks. After a stable recording, the nerve was stimulated “every 30 s to collect 20–100 (average 35) uEPSCs.” | This sentence describes collection of individual uEPSCs. It does not explicitly assign a 30 s repetition interval to the 4 s conditioning plus 500 ms Fig. 8 test trains. It cannot supply an exact inter-train interval or prove complete recovery. |
| Supplement Fig. S8, PDF p. 14 | Separate experiments examine recovery after high-frequency stimulation and pauses in a 7 Hz train. | Those recovery protocols do not specify the preceding history or inter-trial scheduling of Fig. 8D–F. No recovery curve was fitted or substituted here. |

The source does not provide a Fig. 8D–F-specific order of test frequencies, previous high-frequency trains, per-cell trial sequence, complete-recovery check, or exact interval between conditioning/test trains. The stabilization and recruitment procedure itself involves stimulation, but its timing relative to each Fig. 8 trial is unavailable.

For the saved calibration, the first-test efficacy `a0 ≈ 0.0097671` is a consequence of the chosen fully recovered start, exact 28-event baseline and fitted parameters. It is not a measured physiological baseline. The normalized shape alone cannot validate that initial state. The generic 30 s collection statement does not resolve this limitation, especially for a fitted recovery time at the 100 s engineering bound. No additional initial state, phase or recovery parameter was fitted in this follow-up.
