# Local airflow and plume resolution

The body now exposes airflow at the same two funiculus body origins used for odor sampling. MuJoCo point Jacobians include translation, rotation and articulated joint velocity. Subtracting each sensor velocity from the world air velocity gives the flow relative to that antenna. This is then expressed in a head-fixed forward/left/up basis. Imported head mesh axes are not anatomical: the basis is calibrated to the neutral keyframe's thorax axes and follows subsequent head rotation.

`body.observe()["wind"]` contains the two relative velocity vectors in mm/s, their horizontal magnitudes, and wind-source azimuths. Following Suver's convention, source azimuth is 0 anterior, −90° left and +90° right. It points opposite the air velocity, and is undefined (`null`) for zero horizontal flow. The full vectors retain vertical flow. These observations contain no food location or target bearing.

This is physical sensing geometry, not a neural transducer or an antennal aerodynamic model. It does not apply aerodynamic forces to the body, solve near-body flow, or move the antenna according to drag. Suver's empirical passive-deflection curve can supply a separate constrained assay only within its measured speed, angle and preparation domain. The default 2 mm/s arena flow is far below that study's nominal 595.7 mm/s calibration.

The Gaussian puff field now allows explicit emission interval, initialization history and lifetime. Emission refinement preserves a source rate of 10 arbitrary mass units/s at full strength: each puff has mass `10 * interval_s`. Integer emission indices prevent repeated-addition clock drift. Defaults preserve the original 10 Hz/3 s/8 s approximation. Boundaries remain open to odor while walls contain the body; this is not a sealed wind tunnel or CFD simulation.

At 595.7 mm/s, a 100 ms emission interval places adjacent puffs almost 60 mm apart, larger than the arena. A separate fast-wind assay therefore requires a finer interval and an explicitly declared retention window. Changing wind speed alone can cause spurious intermittency. Resolution and mass-flux tests are engineering validation, not measurements of real odor turbulence.

Executed resolution check: constant flow at 595.7 mm/s, a sensor 10 mm downwind and 0.7 mm above the floor, source height 0.07 mm, 100 ms prehistory and 200 ms retention. Across 101 samples in 10 ms, the 100 ms emission interval produced a mean of 1,645.68 arbitrary concentration units, versus 3,975,097.30 at 1 ms and 0.5 ms. The two finer histories agreed to relative tolerance `1e-8`; the coarse history missed most of the passing signal. All intervals emitted the same mass over a complete 100 ms source window. This establishes the need for temporal refinement in this Gaussian model; it does not identify a biological odor concentration scale.

Validation in `tests/test_wind.py` checks source-direction convention, invariance under a common translation or rotation, and actual MuJoCo antenna velocity against an independent position finite difference with simultaneous base and joint motion. [MuJoCo Jacobian API](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/APIreference/APIfunctions.html#mj-jacbody) documents the point-velocity calculation. The measured wind-data fit has a separate evidence record.
