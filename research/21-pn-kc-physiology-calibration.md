# PN→KC physiology: a bounded first calibration assay

2026-09-05. Source audit for [the PN–KC–APL plan](../docs/pn-kc-apl-calibration-plan.md); no model run, fit, figure digitization or reinterpretation of the intervention trials. Exact source files, acquisition outcomes and observed source discrepancies are pinned in [the receipt](../validation/pn-kc-physiology-source-review.json). Papers and figure renders are retained under ignored `data/raw/pn-kc-physiology/`.

**Recommendation: begin with a single-event KC EPSP/EPSC compatibility assay from Turner et al. (2008), with the intrinsic threshold and APL excluded from the fit.** It supplies printed voltage-amplitude and temporal targets immediately. Gruntman & Turner (2013) supplies the next, stronger integration assay, but its exact PN spike histories and numerical per-cell responses are not publicly linked in the inspected article/supplement. Its light durations are not specified PN spike trains. Neither paper supplies a male, VM7d-specific transfer function.

## 1. Immediately usable measurements

[Turner, Bazhenov & Laurent, 2008, *J Neurophysiol* 99:734–746, DOI 10.1152/jn.01283.2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18094099/); [public author PDF](https://www.bazhlab.ucsd.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/JNeurophys2008.pdf). Methods pp.735–736; Fig.3 and results pp.739–740.

All flies were Canton-S **females**. KCs: 1–2 days after eclosion; PNs: 4–12 days. In-vivo somatic whole-cell recording, dye-confirmed identity; current-clamp signals filtered at 3 kHz and sampled at 10 kHz. The study includes α/β, α′/β′ and γ KCs, but does **not** give a subclass or exact PN identity for each Fig.3 physiological cohort. The table is not a modern MaleCNS subtype calibration.

| Observable | Reported measurement | Population / interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| KC holding potential | −58 ± 2 mV, explicitly SD | A recording condition, not an independently measured unforced resting potential for every KC |
| Somatic input resistance | >10 GΩ | Bound, not a fitted population mean |
| Somatic membrane time constant from hyperpolarizing current injection | >200 ms | Separate observable from synaptic EPSP decay; exact current protocol, individual values and uncertainty are not supplied |
| Spontaneous EPSP amplitude | 1.4 ± 0.8 mV; median 1.2 mV | Detected spontaneous events; not an identified PN→KC pair or one graph contact. Coincident/multiquantal events can contribute |
| Miniature EPSP amplitude with 1 μM TTX | 1.0 ± 0.5 mV | Does not establish a one-PN-spike amplitude; amplitude event denominator not explicitly given |
| EPSP 10–90% rise; exponential decay | 2.1 ± 0.5 ms; 11.5 ± 5.3 ms | Seven KC recordings. Methods: 49 events, seven/cell; Results: 50 events. Preserve this discrepancy |
| EPSC 10–90% rise; exponential decay | 0.9 ± 0.4 ms; 2.8 ± 1.2 ms | Voltage clamp at −60 mV; 50 events, ten/cell, five flies. Fig.3D has no absolute current-amplitude scale |
| Holding/rest-to-spike threshold distance | 21.5 ± 5.6 mV, n=17 KCs | Odor responses with ≤4 spikes; threshold is peak second derivative of voltage. Authors used mean holding −57.8 mV to set their model threshold −36.3 mV |
| Baseline KC firing | 0.1 ± 0.4 spikes/s | Whole-cell cohort summary; not a hard ceiling or a subclass distribution |
| Baseline EPSP event rate / PN firing rate | 32.6 ± 12.7 events/s, n=27 KCs; 4.6 ± 4.2 spikes/s, n=37 PNs | Separate recordings, not paired transmission probability |

Except where SD is explicitly identified, `±` above is preserved as **reported dispersion**: the inspected text/caption does not explicitly label each statistic as SD or SEM. Do not turn these ranges into confidence intervals, per-cell bounds or weighted fitting errors. The means are quantitative summary targets; the underlying event/cell data and covariance are unavailable here.

The paper's estimated convergence (~7 from the EPSP/PN rate ratio; anatomical estimate ~13; proposed range 5–15) depends on detection, release probability and anatomical assumptions. It is **not** a direct measurement of 10 independent claws per cell, nor a conversion between connectome synapse counts and physiological weight. Fig.3J is the authors' simulation, not an additional experimental target. The authors explicitly selected an effective model decay to reproduce EPSPs; their model conductance densities and membrane capacitance are model choices, not direct measured total-cell nS/pF.

The odor-response context also stays separate: 500 ms odor, six trials, 22 s interstimulus interval, nominal 1:1,000 final dilution. Response classification used 200 ms bins, >3.5 baseline SD within 0–2 s, and a reliability criterion on at least half the trials. The 6 ± 5% responding fraction across the 71-KC panel is not “any spike in a simulation.” Reported responding α′/β′ KCs emitted 4.9 ± 3.0 spikes versus 2.2 ± 1.2 for α/β; these are response statistics, not a universal rate cap or paired calibration cohorts for Fig.3.

## 2. The next integration assay, and what is actually controlled

[Gruntman & Turner, 2013, *Nat Neurosci* 16:1821–1829, DOI 10.1038/nn.3547](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn.3547); [full author manuscript](https://www.janelia.org/sites/default/files/Labs/nihms524698.pdf), [readable PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3908930/), [journal supplement](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art:10.1038%2Fnn.3547/MediaObjects/41593_2013_BFnn3547_MOESM1_ESM.pdf).

The electrophysiology used **4–7-day-old females**, Mz19-Gal4 × UAS-ChR2-YFP. The driver labels 13 PNs from **DA1, DC3 and VA1d**. Antennae and perineural sheath were removed; retinal feeding and wide-field 470 nm illumination were used. A 1 A command drove the LED: it is neither neuronal injection current nor a PN firing rate. Randomly interleaved light pulses lasted **1, 2, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100 and 250 ms**. PN cell-attached recordings provided actual responses (n=8), with a strong early transient: the text reports **seven PN spikes on average during 25 ms of light**, and continued firing **>100 spikes/s** during the later plateau. Seven/25 ms implies a 280 Hz window average, **not** an observed constant 280 Hz spike train. The 500 pA, 500 ms pulse at the end of PN cell-attached recordings was a seal/break-in check, not the stimulation assay.

| Assay | Measured endpoint | Sample / limitation |
|---|---|---|

| Fig.4A–C, connected-claw integration | Peak of **trial-averaged** voltage minus baseline; early rise and plateau at roughly 30 ms; 1/2-claw responses approximately additive, 3/5-claw responses sublinear | Connected cohort: 17, 14, 6, 2 KCs with 1, 2, 3, 5 connected claws, respectively. Fig.4C bars: SEM; expected response is scaled mean one-claw response, not an independently measured control |
| Fig.4D–E, temporal summation | Observed/expected response to 3 ms somatic current pulse plus 10 ms PN photostimulation at varied offsets | n=7 KCs; plotted offsets span −50 to +50 ms. Current amplitude and full exact stimulus/response arrays are not given in inspected text. No supralinear coincidence boost was demonstrated |
| Supplementary Fig.5C–D | Somatic I–V, nonspiking trials | n=5 KCs; current axis in **pA**, response axis `Vmax − Vbase` in mV. Points are plotted, not supplied as a table; maximum response need not equal steady-state resistance |
| Supplementary Fig.5A–B | Light response vs holding potential | 750 ms current injection with 250 ms light within it. PDF says n=6, journal inline caption says n=4; unresolved, so do not pool or weight this panel |
| Fig.5A, spiking in connected cohort | Baseline-subtracted spikes from light onset through **200 ms after light offset** | n=39; two reliably spiking cells (>0.5 spikes/trial), at 3 and 5 claws. This denominator is selected for connectivity and successful fill |
| Fig.5B–D, separate spiking search | Spikes and anatomical connectivity in responding KCs | 191 screened; 13 reliably responding, adequately filled KCs, with 1/2/3/4/6 claws (2/3/1/6/1 cells). Not a duplicate 39-cell cohort |

A connected claw means a KC claw contacted by **at least one labeled PN bouton**. Complex claws sometimes contact multiple boutons. A graph edge/contact count cannot supply this variable without compartment/morphology mapping. Fig.5D's response probabilities also use a **modeled connectivity denominator** inferred from the first cohort; they are not direct binomial counts for every claw class. Four active claws are therefore neither a universal spike threshold nor an obligatory minimum: two single-claw spiking KCs occurred in the separate 191-cell screen.

The 2–5-day-old, α′/β′-enriched MARCM/GCaMP3 **imaging** cohort is different from these randomly targeted electrophysiological KCs. The authors could not establish a direct conversion of those somatic calcium signals to KC spikes. Do not assign its subtype enrichment or calcium recruitment threshold to the electrophysiological sample. Neither electrophysiology assay reports APL silencing; residual circuit inhibition is not measured separately.

## 3. Source availability and extraction boundary

Both primary articles and the supplement are retained without institutional login. Nature's main page is a subscription preview, but the linked author manuscript is public. Direct PMC PDF access returned a download-challenge HTML page (retained as `.pdf.html`), **not** a PDF; ordinary PMC HTML retrieval succeeded and contains the full methods. No machine-readable trial spike trains, per-cell voltage traces, or source-data workbook is linked in the inspected main articles, supplement or Janelia publication page. This is a bounded finding, not a claim that the authors possess no data.

Turner's Fig.3C–D can support a future graphical extraction plan, but Fig.3D lacks absolute EPSC amplitude. Gruntman's main Figures 3–5 and supplementary plots are embedded raster images (confirmed with `pdfimages -list`); visible curves/points are not lossless raw recordings. Future digitization must preserve pixel/anchor uncertainty, overlap and unidentifiable points. No points or waveforms were digitized here.

Two additional source issues matter before extraction. In the retained Gruntman author PDF and PMC caption, **Figure 3 letters disagree with the image**: actual panel **F** is the PN mean-rate plot, **C** the PN raster, **B** anatomy, **E** connectivity and **G** overlaid KC traces. Locate panels by visible axes/content as well as letters. Also retain the Supplementary Fig.5B n=6/n=4 disagreement above. In Turner, the Fig.3 results paragraph prints a positive “58 ± 2 mV” once, but the earlier recording description, negative voltage axes and stated mean holding −57.8 mV establish the negative holding convention; do not treat the isolated missing minus as a positive-voltage protocol.

## 4. Finite proposed first local reproduction

**First deliverable: an effective single-input waveform compatibility report, not an odor-recruitment fit.** Use one local KC, the unchanged current implementation as its explicit control, and a separately declared candidate; no full network, APL tuning, threshold sweep or spike-count target.

1. At the source holding conditions, deliver one isolated *effective excitatory synaptic event*. Measure baseline-to-peak EPSP, 10–90% rise and decay; in a separate voltage-clamp calculation compare the **normalized** EPSC rise/decay. Score the printed Turner targets above. If a later fit is authorized, restrict it to the effective excitatory event amplitude and its temporal response; retain the current model's error. A prescribed event is an engineering assay input standing in for the recorded spontaneous event, not a measured PN spike of known identity, release probability or graph-contact count.
2. Independently report somatic current-response resistance/time constant and the measured 21.5 mV mean distance to threshold as **constraints**, without fitting them to desired KC sparseness. Do not tune threshold against APL. The >200 ms intrinsic bound and 11.5 ms EPSP decay must remain separate: a simple passive one-compartment, fast-current model cannot make both its intrinsic late decay and its synaptic late decay independent. An effective point-cell approximation may match the synaptic waveform, but then must explicitly fail/defer the intrinsic somatic constraint; a compartmental or other mechanistic extension needs a separate justified design.
3. Reserve Gruntman's first-EPSP distribution and multi-claw voltage/summation curves as **evaluation data not used in this fit**. Their published information has already been inspected, so they are not a blind external validation. A subsequent extraction plan can digitize identifiable per-cell summaries; exact PN timing, release history, KC subclass, and claw-to-contact mapping remain unsupported. Synthetic trains matching seven spikes/25 ms or the plotted mean PN rate must be declared synthetic, not exact repetitions of the biological experiment.

This produces an executable local question with current information: can the selected model class reproduce the measured effective subthreshold response while exposing its intrinsic mismatch? It cannot yet identify absolute PN→KC conductance per contact, capacitance, presynaptic release/depression, a male VM7d transfer function, spike adaptation/refractory parameters, APL strength, or the full odor-ensemble sparseness. Fitting those jointly to a desired recruitment fraction would hide the unresolved quantities rather than calibrate them.
