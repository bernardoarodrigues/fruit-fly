# Mechanism findings from the native-body and olfactory assays

Evidence date: 2026-09-04 Pacific / 2026-09-05 UTC. These are findings about this numerical model and its implementation. None establishes previously unknown biology. Source studies, uncertain transfers, numerical results and proposed explanations remain separate.

## Established in the recorded experiments

1. **Body command withdrawal and neural inactivity are different interventions.** The learned FlyBody policy has residual motion under zero command. A declared source-native posture hold allows the tested stops and restarts while retaining active position/adhesion actuation. It is not evidence for a biological stance circuit. [Comparison](../docs/flybody-motor-comparison.md), [posture experiment](../docs/flybody-stance-experiment.md).
2. **Finite imitation bookkeeping can be removed without changing the tested actor or physics trajectories.** A fixed 66-row rolling reference preserves all 741 actual actor-input values and every recorded physical sample over the two-second comparison. The extra row repairs a stale post-step cache tail that is refreshed before the next actor evaluation. Two twelve-second schedules cross the original finite-recording limits with unchanged physical guards. This result concerns inference infrastructure, not an indefinitely reliable animal. [Experiment](../docs/flybody-persistent-experiment.md), [independent reconstruction](../docs/flybody-persistent-independent-review.md).
3. **Feedback changes the meaning of a paired intervention.** In the actual native-body/full-MaleCNS pair, downstream activity first differs while complete input/RNG histories still match; later, physical feedback causes input histories to diverge. Requiring identical afferents throughout would remove part of the intervention's causal effect. [Four-trial record](../docs/flybody-loop.md).
4. **The on-food native-body trial does not isolate sweet dependence.** Blocking sweet outputs changes graph activity but leaves 28 MN9 spikes and 0.03744 normalized intake unchanged. Odor and club inputs remain active. The earlier isolated tarsal assay used a different body/input configuration and did show dependence. These are different experiments, not interchangeable replications. [Native-body review](../docs/flybody-loop-independent-review.md), [earlier taste assay](../docs/STATUS.md).
5. **Excitation-event rate and afferent-spike rate are distinct model quantities.** In the 36-cell VM7d primary-summary assay, actual ethyl-acetate pulse rates are near the configured summary rate, but requested arrivals, applied voltage events and source spikes differ. Source-output blocking preserves requested events while recurrent effects slightly alter actual source firing. A chemical-response table cannot be declared a spike clamp by relabeling its numbers. [Primary audit](../docs/or42a-primary-assay.md), [executed experiment](../docs/or42a-summary-experiment.md).
6. **Large downstream counts do not demonstrate useful sensory control.** An imposed 11 Hz VM7d baseline alone produces extensive recurrent activity in the transferred Shiu model; the pulse windows retain silent walking groups and implausible voltage extrema. Finite state and correct delayed-edge accounting coexist with physiological failure. Total graph firing therefore cannot serve as an odor-navigation validation score.
7. **Metadata preservation is part of reproducibility.** The native full-loop review recovered every declared runtime/configuration/data-receipt hash, but one pre-run proposal-document digest lacks matching original contents. The mismatch is preserved rather than rewritten. Numerical conclusions rely on the separately retained assay plan, source and raw records; exact preservation of every declared file remains false. [Provenance qualification](../docs/flybody-loop-independent-review.md).

## Hypotheses and discriminating next tests

| Hypothesis about this model | What the existing evidence allows | Test that could distinguish it |
|---|---|---|
| Odor or club feedback can recruit MN9 without sweet input in the native-body assay | Compatible with the unchanged sweet-block endpoint; neither channel has been isolated | Replay the exact recorded input histories with an odor/sweet/club outgoing-block factorial. Compare actual MN9 spike times and Fdg candidates. Keep all input IDs/rates/order and RNG states fixed. Report full feedback trials separately. |
| Strong recurrent propagation under transferred current-based parameters obscures sensory selectivity | Compatible with baseline-driven activity and implausible voltage values; not a fitted physiological diagnosis | Validate class-specific dynamics against independent physiological recordings before optimizing a motor outcome. Compare input/source/downstream timing under fixed measured constraints; retain the replicated Shiu model as a reference. |
| A continuously integrated target can drift far enough under variable neural commands to trip the original body guard | Not excluded by two chosen straight-command schedules | Run predeclared longer full-neural trials and low-speed/turn perturbations with all guards intact, saving the true partial failure state. Do not recenter the target to force completion. |
| Apparent sensory asymmetry can come from geometry or annotation uncertainty | Actual bilateral body-origin sensors and incomplete side/receptor annotations are distinct evidence layers | Audit receptor sampling points and their Jacobian velocities in the physical model, then test controlled rigid translations/rotations before fitting any wind response or attributing asymmetry to a male circuit. |

The next work remains the single-male simulation: calibrated sensory-to-motor behavior, male body mapping, physical environment interactions, visual and wind transduction, oral mechanics and the broader repertoire. The second animal and paired reproduction stay deferred. These engineering findings do not close those milestones.
