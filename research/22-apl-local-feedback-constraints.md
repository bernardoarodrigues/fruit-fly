# APL local-feedback constraints

Source review only, 2026-09-05. Adult APL is described as **non-spiking** in Amin et al. (2020), which attributes absent somatic action potentials to Papadopoulou et al. (2011). Amin explicitly leaves open active conductances elsewhere in the arbor. Its new functional measurements are calcium imaging, not intracellular voltage. A continuous, spatially distributed APL model is therefore a justified candidate; neither this paper nor Lin et al. (2014) identifies an absolute voltage-to-release law. The existing optional graded backend does not establish APL parameters. [Amin, introduction and discussion](https://elifesciences.org/articles/56954).

| Observable | Direct evidence and quantitative scope | Useful constraint; missing conversion |
|---|---|---|
| KC→APL calcium | Lin Fig. 3c: thermal KC activation gives APL GCaMP3 maximum ΔF/F **1.90 ± 0.44 SEM**, n=5; driver-negative control **−0.27 ± 0.05**, n=4. Maxima are evaluated between 30°C and the temperature maximum. | Direction and reporter amplitude under this thermal protocol; no prescribed KC spike train or voltage calibration. |
| KC→APL vesicle release | Lin Fig. 3d: synapto-pHluorin maximum ΔF/F **0.68 ± 0.09 SEM**, n=5; control **0.06 ± 0.007**, n=5. Blocking KC output suppresses odor-evoked APL calcium reversibly. | Separate vesicle-fusion evidence for the feedback path; not GABA concentration, release probability, conductance, or proof of monosynaptic transmission by this assay alone. |
| Spatial APL calcium and KC suppression | Amin Figs. 5–7: local **0.75 mM ATP**, **10 ms at 12.5 psi**, activates APL expressing P2X2 at horizontal lobe, vertical lobe, or calyx. **7.5 mM GABA** is a separate local-application comparison. Signals use GCaMP6f and **20 µm** normalized backbone segments. | Compare profiles and site dependence, retaining the finite stimulus footprint. Co-ejected dye decays to half-strength over approximately **10–25 µm**; ATP/GABA may spread farther. Neither backbone distance nor dye spread is an electrical space constant. |
| Population and behavior | Lin Figs. 5–7: disrupting feedback broadens KC calcium maps and increases similarity between particular odor responses; learned discrimination is measured by the change in time allocated to the unpunished odor after training. | Independent population/behavior tests. Pixel-map sparseness is not a fraction of spiking cells; the behavioral score supplies no instantaneous motor gain. |

The Lin values are **reported caption numbers**, not digitized or newly fitted. Lin used male and female flies aged 1–7 days, with temperature/genotype controls and an exposed, superfused brain for imaging. The temperature-sensitive output block included ≥15 minutes at 32°C and 10–15 odor pulses to deplete vesicles; it is not an instantaneous computational edge deletion. [Lin, Fig. 3 and Online Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn.3660).

For Amin’s odor assay, let `R` denote KC ΔF/F. Fig. 6 defines the inhibitory effect as **(R[odor+ATP] − R[odor]) / max_time(R[odor])**; negative values indicate suppression. Fig. 7 then averages the indicated time windows and additionally scales by the largest absolute response across matched conditions. Its means and SEM must be compared at those same normalization stages. APL calcium: horizontal/vertical stimulation each 10 neurons from 6 flies, calyx 6/4. KC calcium with APL activation: horizontal/vertical each 10/9, calyx 9/8. GABA comparison: horizontal/vertical each 13/8, calyx 11/6. These are separate reporter cohorts. Amin’s main text/methods did not locate a sex or age statement in this review; no sex-specific transfer is assumed.

Amin’s **approximately 50 µm** space constant is an author-fitted phenomenological description using hemibrain morphology, calcium and dye profiles. The corresponding membrane/axial resistivity ratio is approximately **0.025 m** under that model; neither resistivity was measured in APL. It is not a directly measured voltage decay or a MaleCNS parameter. Their distance-weighted KC→APL→KC calculation predicts relative inhibition from anatomy; it omits dynamic feedback and does not measure pairwise KC inhibitory currents. Undetectable calcium does not establish zero voltage or release. The cited 2 mV release sensitivity concerns other insect interneurons, not an APL threshold. Calyx P2X2 leak controls and the discussion of possible selective calcium suppression further prevent a universal calcium→spike conversion. [Amin, Figs. 6–8 and discussion](https://elifesciences.org/articles/56954).

A small future assay could freeze the three stimulus sites, dye footprints, reporter processing, and Fig. 7 comparison windows, then test whether one spatial model predicts both APL calcium localization and KC suppression at a reserved site. Data are already inspected, so this would be a **reserved-condition check**, not a blind validation. Voltage, calcium, release and KC calcium need distinct observation models; absolute conductance, receptor kinetics/reversal, KC→APL unitary input and local membrane properties remain unidentified. No parameter selection, model run or trial-outcome interpretation was performed here.
